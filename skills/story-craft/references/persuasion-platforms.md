## The persuasion foundations

**Kahneman — fast and slow thinking.** Most feed decisions are System 1: fast, intuitive, low-effort. A scroll-stopping ad wins the System 1 moment first — a striking image, a face, a tension, a pattern interrupt — *then* gives System 2 a reason. Don't open with the reasoned argument; open with the thing that earns the half-second of attention, then deliver substance. Cognitive ease (clear, fluent, easy-to-parse messaging) is itself persuasive; friction reads as distrust.

**Cialdini — principles of influence.** Used honestly, these are levers a story can embody rather than state:
- **Social proof** — others like the viewer already use/believe this (testimonials, counts, "join X others").
- **Authority** — credible expertise, credentials, or demonstrated competence.
- **Scarcity** — genuine limited availability or time (only genuine — manufactured scarcity is a dark pattern; see `production-measurement.md`).
- **Commitment & consistency** — a small initial yes leads toward a larger one.
- **Liking** — similarity, warmth, and appeal (ties to Disney's *appeal* and emotional design).
- **Reciprocity** — give value first (a useful tip, a free tool) before asking.

**Narrative transportation** (covered in `story-structures.md`) is the bridge: a transported audience counter-argues less and adopts story-consistent attitudes. Story + an honestly-embodied influence principle beats a bare claim.

## Platform creative codes

These are the current official syntheses from the platforms themselves. They agree more than they differ: hook fast, brand early, message simple, sound-aware, mobile-native.

**Google — ABCDs of effective video.** *Attract* (hook immediately, tight framing, a person/face early), *Brand* (surface the brand early and often, integrated not bolted on), *Connect* (tell a story, make the viewer feel something, keep it focused), *Direct* (one clear call to action). Google's own experiments show early branding and a strong opening move performance.

**TikTok — Creative Codes.** Native, sound-on, fast. Structure as **hook → body → close**. Roughly **90% of ad recall impact lands in the first ~6 seconds**, so the opening must carry the brand and the value. Creator-style authenticity and platform-native music/audio outperform polished-but-foreign ads. A reusable mascot or character system compounds recall across paid and organic.

**Meta — short, mobile-first, branded early.** Feature the brand and key message in the **first ~3 seconds**; design for sound-off (most feed viewing is muted) so the visual carries meaning, with sound as a reinforcing layer. Short runtimes (often 6–15s) for direct-response.

The cross-platform synthesis: **front-load brand and value, keep one focused message, make it work muted, and use sound as reinforcement rather than load-bearing.**

## Duration and pacing by platform

There is no universal right length — match the temporal unit to message complexity and platform norm.

| Format | Practical default | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Short paid social (DR) | ~6–15s; vertical | Hook in ~3s, brand/value early, explicit CTA; works muted |
| YouTube Shorts / vertical ad | under ~60s; ~10–30s for action goals | Vertical, social-first; don't repurpose a horizontal TVC unchanged |
| Brand film (upper funnel) | longer, but earn every second | Emotional arc over feature-dump; small human moment → brand-coded payoff |
| Explainer (owned channels) | as long as the idea needs, segmented | One proposition per beat; let the structure breathe |

## Building for brand recall

Recall is built by **distinctive, repeated brand codes**, not by a single beautiful execution. The levers:

- **A distinctive visual system** — consistent palette, shape language, and treatment the audience starts to recognize across pieces (Kurzgesagt's stable visual grammar is the textbook case — consistency of language matters as much as the motion).
- **A mascot or character device** — a reusable character compresses recall and emotion efficiently and travels across paid and organic (the LikeCard TikTok mascot, the Dumb Ways to Die characters).
- **An audio mnemonic** — a sonic signature or repeatable musical hook; audio measurably lifts recall and emotion when it reinforces the message.
- **Modular, sequenced creative** — build one campaign as many re-cuttable micro-scenes around a single promise, test multiple openings, and sequence by audience. Sofacompany's modular "Sofa Stories" approach (testing openings and lengths, sequencing journeys) produced large uplifts in view rate, brand awareness, and search lift — the lesson is to build for re-cutting and test openings aggressively before scaling.

The trap to name explicitly: **beautiful but weak branding.** A film that wins awards but leaves no brand trace failed the brief. If the brand were swapped out, would anyone notice? If not, the brand codes aren't doing their job.

## The persuasion-creative checklist

- [ ] Does the first ~3s earn the System-1 stop *and* surface the brand/value?
- [ ] Is there one focused message, not three?
- [ ] Does it work fully muted, with sound as reinforcement?
- [ ] Is there exactly one clear, directive CTA?
- [ ] Are brand codes (visual system, character, audio) distinctive and repeated?
- [ ] Is any influence lever (scarcity, social proof) genuine rather than manufactured?
- [ ] Is the creative modular enough to re-cut for other placements and to A/B test openings?
- [ ] Are we measuring true outcomes (recall, lift, conversion) and not just view-through?
